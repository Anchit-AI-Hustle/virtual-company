# Rally Rounds business case

Author: Meera, Research. Date: 2026-11-14. FX used: USD 1 = INR 94.2754 (company/config.json, source xflowpay, 2026-11-14). I did not propose this idea and gain nothing if it passes.

## Summary

- The need is real and it repeats: US pickleball has 24.3 million players and 7.5 million core players who play 8 or more times a year [1], and organisers run round robins every week.
- The exact pitch (browser based, no install, no sign-up, live scores) is already given away free. PicklePal says "Free · no sign-up · works offline" with live scoring and a leaderboard [6]. Bounce offers a "Free, no signup" generator [7]. Bracket Maker says it tracks scores and standings for free [8]. Reclub is a free app with live scores [5].
- People do pay in this niche, but to brands with networks (Pickleheads Pro USD 14.99 a month, extra round robins USD 5 each [2]; Pickleball.com Game Maker Premium USD 4.99 a month [9]). A new, unknown no-install tool has no evidence of anyone paying for it.
- The company has no payment rail (ap-2 decided "do nothing for now" [17]), so revenue is 0 until the owner opens one. Organic search is crowded and the company may not post in communities as the owner.
- Score 14 of 35. Recommendation: no-go.

## Market

**Who pays.** Volunteer and semi-pro organisers of recurring pickleball sessions (club open play, ladders, weekly round robins), mainly in the US. This is a USD market. Pricing in INR would aim at a market with no sourced demand; I found no India-specific evidence in this session.

**How many (sourced):**
- 24.3 million Americans aged 6 and over played pickleball in 2025, up from 4.2 million in 2020 [1].
- About 7.5 million were CORE players, defined as playing 8 or more times in the year [1].
- One Pickleheads testimonial mentions "nearly 500 players at our facility" [4]. That is one facility, not a market figure.

**What is unknown.** The number of people who organise round robins, and how many run them often enough to pay. SFIA gives no figure on organised play [1]. I found no primary source for the organiser count, so it is unknown. Padel was named in the venture's customer line, but I found no evidence for padel round robin demand in this session, so it is not counted.

## Problem

- **Pain.** Real but well served. Building fair rotations, handling odd numbers and sit-outs, and keeping scores is fiddly by hand, which is why so many tools exist. The pain is solved for free today by several tools [5][6][7][8], so the remaining pain is small.
- **Frequency.** Repeats. Organisers run sessions weekly or more, so a subscription fits the behaviour, and competitors sell monthly plans [2][9][10].
- **Install friction (the claimed gap).** Pickleheads says its round robin tool is "available exclusively in our top rated mobile app" [4], and Reclub, Game Maker and Dink are apps [5][9][12]. So "no install" is a real difference from the leaders. But it is not a difference from PicklePal, Bounce's generator or Bracket Maker, which already run in the browser [6][7][8].
- I found no direct complaint thread from organisers about having to make players install an app; a search for such complaints returned only vendor pages. Evidence for this pain is therefore missing.

## Willingness to pay

**For:**
- Organisers pay Pickleheads USD 14.99 a month for Pro, and USD 5 per extra round robin [2].
- Pickleball.com charges USD 4.99 a month or USD 49.99 a year for organised formats [9].
- Small apps sell lifetime or annual unlocks: Dink at USD 24.99 a year or USD 39.99 once [12], DinkDrop up to USD 44.99 [13].
- PickleSync lists USD 19 and 49 a month plans [10]. Its sales volume is unknown.

**Against:**
- The small paid apps show little traction: 17 ratings for Dink [12], 11 for DinkDrop [13], 3 for Pickleball Scheduler [14]. Actual revenue for any of them is unknown.
- The money goes where the players already are (Pickleheads with 9.2k reviews [4], Pickleball.com [9]). Paying there buys a player network, which Rally Rounds would not have.
- A direct browser competitor, PicklePal, has not yet launched its paid tier and calls USD 9 a month a "planned price" [6]. Nobody has shown that a no-install tool can charge.
- No evidence was found of any organiser paying for a no-install, no-network tool.

Currency: USD. Any price should be in USD; an INR price has no supporting evidence.

## Distribution

Limits: no paid ads, no posting as the owner, no messages to strangers. That leaves organic search, marketplaces and communities that welcome tool posts.

- **Organic search.** Demand exists: Google autocomplete for "pickleball round robin" returns "generator", "generator free", "charts printable pdf", "schedule generator" and "for 8 players" [15]. Volumes are unknown (no keyword tool was available). The intent is strongly "free" and "printable", which is the opposite of a paid product. The results already hold Pickleheads, Bounce, Bracket Maker, visu.network and others [18]. A new domain ranking for these within 90 days is unlikely; how unlikely is unknown.
- **Communities.** r/Pickleball has roughly 152K members [16], but I could not confirm its self-promotion rules [16]. The company has no community accounts of its own, and posting as the owner is forbidden. This channel is unproven.
- **Marketplaces.** App stores are where competitors sell [12][13], but a no-install web product does not sit there, which removes the channel where this niche's buyers browse.
- **Viral loop.** Players open a shared link to see scores. That exposes the tool to every player in a session, and it is the only channel that does not need ads or outreach. Whether it converts players into organisers is unknown.

Verdict: weak and crowded. Score 2.

## Unit economics

Costs: hosting on the existing Vercel team and the existing domain, assumed 0 extra at this scale; payment fees depend on a rail that does not exist yet, so they are unknown. Build time is agent time.

The figures below are estimates with stated assumptions, not data. Price assumed: USD 4.99 a month organiser pass (matching Game Maker Premium [9]). Paid months in the window assumed at 1.5 on average.

- **Worst: INR 0.** No payment rail exists [17]. If the owner does not open one, nothing can be sold.
- **Likely: about INR 2,100.** Assume a rail by day 45, 300 organiser visits from organic search in the window, 1% conversion: 3 buyers × USD 4.99 × 1.5 months = USD 22.46 = INR 2,117.
- **Best: about INR 31,800.** Assume 1,500 organiser visits, 3% conversion: 45 buyers × USD 4.99 × 1.5 months = USD 336.83 = INR 31,754.

Even the best case is small, and it rests on visit and conversion numbers I cannot source.

## Risks

What would prove this case wrong, so the decision can be revisited:
- If, by 2026-12-31, a no-install browser tool (PicklePal Club Pro or similar [6]) shows real paying organisers, the willingness to pay score should rise.
- If the owner opens a payment rail and a demand test page gets 10 or more organiser sign-ups for a paid pass within 30 days of going live, the case should be reopened.

What would hurt a build:
- Pickleheads or Pickleball.com add a no-install web view for players. Pickleheads already lets anyone "try a test round robin" [4]. That would erase the only gap.
- Free browser tools keep improving at no cost to organisers [6][7][8].
- No payment rail [17]; collecting USD needs an account in the owner's name.
- Search results are full of established pages [18]; a new site may get near 0 visits for months. The number is unknown.

## Options

- **Proceed:** build a free no-install round robin with a USD 4.99 a month pass. Faces free like-for-like competitors and no payment rail. Not advised.
- **Pivot:** only if a narrower buyer appears with evidence, for example padel or tennis social round robins outside the US if a sourced gap is found. No evidence for this yet.
- **Stop (do nothing for now):** record the research, keep the evidence, revisit if the risk triggers above fire.
